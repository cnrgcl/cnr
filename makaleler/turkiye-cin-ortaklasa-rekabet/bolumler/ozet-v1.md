## Abstract

This study examines in which domains Türkiye and China cooperate and in which they compete over the tourism heritage of the Silk Road, drawing on coopetition theory. The separation principle, developed at firm and destination level, predicts that cooperation concentrates in activities far from the customer and competition in activities close to the customer; the study transfers this prediction to inter-state heritage tourism. In a single embedded case design, 49 official documents from 2010–2026 and the 368 meaning units extracted from them were coded by two independent coders, using a scheme based on manifest markers, across four domains: connectivity, tourist flows, narrative and value capture. The Turkish Statistical Institute series on arrivals of Chinese nationals was read descriptively. ## Extended Summary

### Purpose

Official discourse between Türkiye and China presents the Silk Road as a shared heritage and tourism as a natural field of cooperation. Türkiye aligned its Middle Corridor with the Belt and Road Initiative through a memorandum signed in Antalya in 2015 (Resmî Gazete, 2017), 2018 was celebrated as the Year of Turkish Tourism in China (Niu & Li, 2019), the two culture and tourism ministries signed a tourism cooperation memorandum in 2024, and from 2 January 2026 Türkiye granted Chinese citizens unilateral visa-free entry (Türkiye Turizm Tanıtım ve Geliştirme Ajansı [TGA], 2026). The Turkish tourism literature reads this relationship largely as a win-win partnership and treats its problems, such as security perceptions, language and distance, as technical obstacles (İbiş, 2019; Kılıç, 2021; Palidan, 2024). Heritage politics research, however, shows that "shared" heritage also generates competing claims of ownership and centrality (Brown & O'Brien, 2025; Debarbieux et al., 2023), and China's historicisation of the Silk Road has been described as Sinocentric (Sciorati, 2023). The study asks two questions. First, in which domains do Türkiye and China cooperate and in which do they compete over Silk Road tourism heritage between 2010 and 2026, and does this distribution follow the activity's proximity to the tourist? Second, to what extent have the two countries' cooperation discourse and agreements been reflected in arrivals of Chinese nationals to Türkiye?

### Methodology

The study adopts a critical realist stance and a single embedded case design, with the Türkiye–China dyad as the case and four domains, ordered in advance by proximity to the tourist, as embedded units: connectivity, tourist flows, narrative and value capture. The theoretical mechanism tested is the separation principle derived from Bengtsson and Kock (2000, p. 421), according to which competitors cooperate in input activities far from the customer and compete in output activities close to the customer. Systematic document analysis (Bowen, 2009) was applied to two corpora: 25 bilateral or joint official documents (Corpus A) and 24 unilateral Silk Road promotion and policy narratives (Corpus B), collected through criterion sampling from Turkish and Chinese official sources. The documents were segmented into 368 meaning units. Orientation was derived mechanically from three manifest markers (a joint subject, self-promotion, and exclusion or one-sidedness) to produce cooperation, competition, mixed or neutral codes. A pilot round showed that interpretive judgement produced very low agreement, which led to the marker-based scheme; pilot codes were not used in the analysis. Final coding was carried out by two independent human coders, and inter-coder agreement is reported with Cohen's kappa [[κ domain = __; κ orientation = __]]. The decision rule for the separation principle was written before the analysis: a monotonic increase in the share of competition codes from connectivity to value capture, or a difference of at least 20 percentage points between the two ends, counts as support. Arrivals of Chinese nationals from the Turkish Statistical Institute series for 2010–2025 were used descriptively, without causal testing (Türkiye İstatistik Kurumu [TÜİK], 2026).

### Originality and Value

The study makes three contributions. Theoretically, it carries the separation principle, so far examined among firms and destinations (Chim-Miki & Batista-Canino, 2017a; Wang & Krakover, 2008), to heritage tourism between two states. Existing work on Silk Road coopetition addresses multilateral programme management (Redi & Pulido Fernández, 2018), and international relations studies of the Türkiye–China dyad or of great power coopetition explain the relationship as a whole without asking in which activities cooperation and competition concentrate (Wang & Sun, 2024; Wisniewski, 2025). Conceptually, it shows that the discourse of a "shared Silk Road" can carry partnership and ownership claims at the same time, and proposes that the Silk Road heritage is the object of coopetition rather than a neutral common asset. Practically, it offers a framework for identifying where Türkiye's China tourism policy gains from cooperation and where it needs differentiation.
