## Abstract

Türkiye and China share a strong discourse of cooperation that presents the Silk Road as a common heritage; yet although arrivals of Chinese nationals in Türkiye tripled between 2013 and 2025, they never exceeded one per cent of all foreign arrivals. While the literature explains this gap by obstacles to cooperation, this study looks at the relationship itself and uses coopetition theory to ask whether the two countries also compete over the heritage they share. According to the separation principle of the theory, cooperation concentrates in activities far from the customer and competition in activities close to the customer. To test this prediction, 49 official documents from 2010–2026 were divided into 368 meaning units, which two independent coders coded across the domains of connectivity, tourist flows, narrative and value capture using a scheme based on manifest markers. ## Extended Summary

### Purpose

Official discourse between Türkiye and China presents the Silk Road as a shared heritage and tourism as a natural field of cooperation. Recent steps follow this narrative: Türkiye aligned its Middle Corridor with the Belt and Road Initiative through a memorandum signed in Antalya in 2015 (Resmî Gazete, 2017), 2018 was celebrated as the Year of Turkish Tourism in China (Niu & Li, 2019), the two culture and tourism ministries signed a tourism cooperation memorandum in 2024, and from 2 January 2026 Türkiye granted Chinese citizens unilateral visa-free entry (Türkiye Turizm Tanıtım ve Geliştirme Ajansı [TGA], 2026). These steps create a clear expectation that Chinese tourists will follow, but the data meet this expectation only in part. Arrivals of Chinese nationals tripled after 2013, yet they never exceeded one per cent of all foreign arrivals (Türkiye İstatistik Kurumu [TÜİK], 2026). The Turkish tourism literature explains this gap by technical obstacles such as security perceptions, language and distance, because it treats the relationship as a win-win partnership from the outset (İbiş, 2019; Kılıç, 2021; Palidan, 2024). Heritage politics research suggests a different reading: "shared" heritage also generates competing claims of ownership and centrality (Brown & O'Brien, 2025; Debarbieux et al., 2023), and China's historicisation of the Silk Road has been described as Sinocentric (Sciorati, 2023). If both readings hold, the problem may lie in the relationship itself rather than in its obstacles. The study therefore asks two questions. First, in which domains do Türkiye and China cooperate, and in which do they compete, over Silk Road tourism heritage between 2010 and 2026, and does this distribution follow the activity's proximity to the tourist? Second, to what extent have the two countries' cooperation discourse and agreements been reflected in arrivals of Chinese nationals?

### Methodology

Because these questions look for a mechanism, the study adopts a critical realist stance and treats documentary traces as indicators of that mechanism rather than as the mechanism itself. The mechanism tested is the separation principle derived from Bengtsson and Kock (2000, p. 421), according to which competitors cooperate in input activities far from the customer and compete in output activities close to the customer. To compare this mechanism across the parts of a single relationship, a single embedded case design was chosen, with the Türkiye–China dyad as the case and four domains, ordered in advance by proximity to the tourist, as embedded units: connectivity, tourist flows, narrative and value capture. Since cooperation is expected to appear in joint documents and competition in unilateral ones, systematic document analysis (Bowen, 2009) was applied to two corpora: 25 bilateral or joint official documents (Corpus A) and 24 unilateral Silk Road promotion and policy narratives (Corpus B), selected through criterion sampling. The documents were divided into 368 meaning units. A pilot round showed that interpretive coding of orientation produced very low agreement, so the final scheme derives orientation mechanically from three manifest markers (a joint subject, self-promotion, and exclusion or one-sidedness); pilot codes were not used in the analysis. Two independent human coders carried out the final coding, and agreement is reported with Cohen's kappa [[κ domain = __; κ orientation = __]]. To prevent post hoc interpretation, the decision rule was written before the analysis: a monotonic increase in the share of competition codes from connectivity to value capture, or a difference of at least 20 percentage points between the two ends, counts as support. Arrivals of Chinese nationals for 2010–2025 were then read descriptively, without causal testing.

### Originality and Value

The study contributes at three levels. Theoretically, it carries the separation principle, so far examined among firms and destinations (Chim-Miki & Batista-Canino, 2017a; Wang & Krakover, 2008), to heritage tourism between two states. This extension matters because the closest existing work addresses the multilateral management of the Silk Road Programme (Redi & Pulido Fernández, 2018), while international relations studies of the Türkiye–China dyad and of great power coopetition explain the relationship as a whole without asking in which activities cooperation and competition concentrate (Wang & Sun, 2024; Wisniewski, 2025). Conceptually, the study shows that the discourse of a "shared Silk Road" can carry partnership and ownership claims at the same time; for this reason it treats the Silk Road heritage as the object of coopetition rather than as a neutral common asset. Practically, it offers Türkiye's China tourism policy a way to distinguish the domains in which cooperation pays from those in which differentiation is needed.
