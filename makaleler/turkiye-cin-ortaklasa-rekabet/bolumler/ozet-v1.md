## Öz

Türkiye ile Çin, İpek Yolu'nu ortak bir miras olarak sunan güçlü bir iş birliği söylemine sahiptir; buna karşın Çin uyruklu girişler 2013–2025 döneminde üç katına çıkmasına rağmen Türkiye'ye yapılan toplam yabancı girişlerin hiçbir yıl yüzde birini aşmamıştır. Literatür bu açığı iş birliğinin önündeki engellerle açıklarken, bu çalışma ilişkinin kendisine bakmakta ve iki ülkenin aynı mirası paylaşırken onun üzerinde rekabet de edip etmediğini ortaklaşa rekabet kuramıyla incelemektedir. Kuramın ayrıştırma ilkesine göre iş birliği müşteriden uzak, rekabet müşteriye yakın faaliyetlerde yoğunlaşır. Bu öngörüyü sınamak için 2010–2026 dönemine ait 49 resmî belge 368 anlam birimine ayrılmış ve bağlantısallık, turist akışı, anlatı ve değer yakalama alanlarında iki bağımsız kodlayıcı tarafından açık işaretlere dayalı bir şemayla kodlanmıştır. İkili belgelerde iş birliği dili baskındır; ancak rekabet payı turiste yaklaştıkça düzenli olarak artmamakta ve ayrıştırma ilkesi desteklenmemektedir. Var olan rekabet büyük ölçüde Türkiye'nin kendini İpek Yolu'nun merkezi ve kavşağı olarak sunmasından kaynaklanmakta, Çin kaynakları ise ortaklık dilini korumaktadır. Sonuç olarak İpek Yolu mirası ortaklaşa rekabetin nesnesidir, ancak bu rekabet tek taraflıdır; iki ülke arasındaki uzaklık ise hem iş birliğinin baskınlığını hem de söylemin turiste dönüşmemesini açıklamaktadır.

**Anahtar Kelimeler:** Ortaklaşa Rekabet, İpek Yolu Turizmi, Miras Diplomasisi, Türkiye–Çin İlişkileri, Ayrıştırma İlkesi

## Abstract

Türkiye and China share a strong discourse of cooperation that presents the Silk Road as a common heritage; yet although arrivals of Chinese nationals in Türkiye tripled between 2013 and 2025, they never exceeded one per cent of all foreign arrivals. Rather than explaining this gap by obstacles to cooperation, this study asks, through coopetition theory, whether the two countries also compete over the heritage they share. The theory's separation principle predicts cooperation in activities far from the customer and competition in activities close to it. To test this, 49 official documents from 2010–2026 were divided into 368 meaning units, which two independent coders coded across connectivity, tourist flows, narrative and value capture. Cooperation dominates bilateral documents, but competition does not rise steadily with proximity to the tourist, so the separation principle is not supported. The competition that does appear stems largely from Türkiye presenting itself as the centre and crossroads of the Silk Road, while Chinese sources keep a language of partnership. The Silk Road heritage is thus the object of a one-sided coopetition, and distance explains both the dominance of cooperation and its failure to generate tourist flows.

**Keywords:** Coopetition, Silk Road Tourism, Heritage Diplomacy, Türkiye–China Relations, Separation Principle

## Extended Summary

### Purpose

Official discourse between Türkiye and China presents the Silk Road as a shared heritage and tourism as a natural field of cooperation: the Middle Corridor was aligned with the Belt and Road Initiative in 2015 (Resmî Gazete, 2017), 2018 was the Year of Turkish Tourism in China (Niu & Li, 2019), and from 2 January 2026 Türkiye granted Chinese citizens unilateral visa-free entry (Türkiye Turizm Tanıtım ve Geliştirme Ajansı [TGA], 2026). Yet arrivals of Chinese nationals never exceeded one per cent of all foreign arrivals (Türkiye İstatistik Kurumu [TÜİK], 2026). The Turkish literature explains this gap by technical obstacles because it treats the relationship as a win-win partnership (İbiş, 2019; Kılıç, 2021; Palidan, 2024), whereas heritage politics research shows that "shared" heritage also produces competing claims of ownership (Brown & O'Brien, 2025; Debarbieux et al., 2023). The study therefore asks in which domains the two countries cooperate and compete over Silk Road tourism heritage, whether this follows proximity to the tourist, and how far the cooperation discourse is reflected in arrivals.

### Methodology

The study adopts a critical realist stance and a single embedded case design. It tests the separation principle of Bengtsson and Kock (2000, p. 421), according to which competitors cooperate far from the customer and compete close to it, across four domains ordered in advance by proximity to the tourist: connectivity, tourist flows, narrative and value capture. Systematic document analysis (Bowen, 2009) was applied to 25 bilateral and 24 unilateral official documents, divided into 368 meaning units. Orientation was derived mechanically from three manifest markers: a joint subject, self-promotion, and exclusion. Two independent human coders coded all units (κ = 0.73 for domain, 0.47 for orientation); disagreements were resolved by majority with an independent AI-assisted coding produced before the human coding, and results are also reported for each coder and for agreed units. The decision rule was written before the analysis: a monotonic rise in competition, or a gap of at least 20 percentage points between the two ends, counts as support. Arrivals for 2010–2025 were read descriptively.

### Findings

Cooperation dominates bilateral documents: 49.7% of their units carry cooperation codes and 20.3% competition codes (P1 supported). In unilateral documents cooperation falls to 27.4% while competition stays at 21.5%, the decline being absorbed by neutral statements (P2 partially supported). Excluding neutral units, competition accounts for 31.8% in connectivity, 36.6% in tourist flows, 32.8% in narrative and 44.4% in value capture, the last resting on only 20 units; the series is not monotonic and the gap stays below the threshold, so P3 is not supported under any coding set. Competition is strongly asymmetric: in Turkish sources it rises from 45.0% to 75.0% across the domains, whereas in Chinese sources it stays between 13.0% and 25.0%. Turkish sources present Türkiye as a bridge, crossroads or hub about twice as often as Chinese sources promote themselves; even the Turkish ambassador's interview in a Chinese magazine stressed that the new Istanbul Airport would be the world's largest. Arrivals rose from 138,876 in 2013 to 425,348 in 2025, but their share peaked at 0.998% in 2018 (TÜİK, 2026). This limited and one-sided competition cannot explain the gap, so P5 is not supported; distance is the more plausible explanation.

### Conclusions

The separation principle travels to inter-state heritage tourism only in part. Türkiye–China coopetition over the Silk Road appears as a one-sided discourse: Türkiye seeks to be the centre, while China keeps to partnership. Value capture is almost absent because states do not discuss money in official texts. Distance explains both the dominance of cooperation and its failure to bring tourists, consistent with evidence that distance reduces Chinese outbound flows (Deng & Hu, 2019). A language of shared heritage is likely to reach Chinese tourists better than an emphasis on Türkiye as a hub, and more direct flights are the most concrete way to shorten the distance. The findings are limited by the selection of official documents, partial reliance on translation, the lack of revenue data and the use of border records rather than tourist counts.

### Originality and Value

The study is the first to carry the separation principle, so far examined among firms and destinations (Chim-Miki & Batista-Canino, 2017a; Wang & Krakover, 2008), to heritage tourism between two states. Existing work addresses the multilateral Silk Road Programme (Redi & Pulido Fernández, 2018) or the Türkiye–China relationship as a whole (Wang & Sun, 2024). By showing that a "shared Silk Road" can carry partnership and ownership claims at once, the study treats the heritage as the object of a one-sided coopetition rather than as a neutral common asset.
